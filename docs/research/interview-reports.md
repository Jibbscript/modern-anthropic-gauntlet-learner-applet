# Reported Anthropic interview patterns

Public candidate reports (Glassdoor, Blind, LeetCode Discuss, Reddit, prep sites, write-ups) about the Anthropic interview loop, gathered and fact-checked on 2026-10-05. None of this is official; attribute it to candidate reports in lessons. Formats vary by role and change over time. The user-provided summary article (2026) is consistent with these reports: culture round feared most; practical progressive coding (image pipelines, concurrent crawlers, stack traces, caches, dedup, interpreters); design in a shared doc (e.g. a Collaborative Prompt Playground); AI-free take-homes unless stated, plus newer agentic rounds.

## Research summary

Reported Anthropic SWE / infra / research-engineer interview process for 2025-2026, compiled from candidate reports (PracHub, Aced/Exponent, Taro, hack2hire, screna), prep sites (Hello Interview, interviewing.io, calibrd, aceoffer, fastprep, The Forward Deployed), press (Axios Aug 2026, Bloomberg via Gigazine/Decoder/implicator, TechCrunch) and Anthropic's own pages.

The usual pipeline:
1. Recruiter screen, 15-30 minutes. It increasingly includes 20 or more minutes of probing on AI safety and "why Anthropic".
2. CodeSignal Industry Coding Assessment, 90 minutes. It is one project built over 4 progressive levels and scored 200-600. Some 2026 reports describe 6 levels. Common projects are an in-memory key-field-value DB, a bank system, cloud storage, a worker time-tracker, a recipe manager and a task manager.
3. Live 50-60 minute practical coding screen in Colab, Replit or CodeSignal. Python is the default.
4. Virtual onsite of 4-5 one-hour sessions: practical coding, system design in a Google Doc (sometimes reviewing a flawed design doc), a hiring-manager round, a project deep dive (about 20 minutes presenting, then discussion) and a culture/values round. The culture round is reported to be where most technically strong candidates fail. The onsite is sometimes split into two gated halves.
5. References and team matching. This stage can be silent for weeks.

The coding questions come from a small, recurring bank:
- A same-host web crawler that then has to be made concurrent.
- Turning stack samples into start/end events, with follow-ups on recursion and debouncing.
- Finding duplicate files (size, then partial hash, then full hash).
- An LRU/memoization cache with *args/**kwargs keys, then persistence through a write-ahead log or pickle.
- A JSON-driven image transform pipeline with PIL that is then parallelized.
- Computing mode, median and sort across 10 nodes using send/recv.
- A greedy longest-match tokenizer.
- Less common: a bootloader loop-repair problem and a streaming token-cost calculator.

Concurrency follow-ups come up in nearly every coding round. System design centres on LLM inference: batching for a GPU that takes 100 inputs per batch, a batched-inference design-doc review at 10k QPS, routing a large and a small model on an 8-GPU pool, distributing 500 GB of weights to about 1,000 GPU workers, and a prompt playground with prompts over 10 MB. Other reported prompts are 1:1 chat, a telemetry pipeline, a rate limiter and guardrails.

Anthropic's official policy (updated Jul 10, 2025) bans AI in take-homes and live interviews unless the instructions say otherwise. The performance-engineering take-home is an explicit exception. In mid-2026 there are some reports of an onsite round where Claude Code was provided to review and improve 4 TypeScript PRs, and of an agent/tool-use coding round in Colab.

## Caveats from the researcher

Access limits: Glassdoor, Blind, Reddit, 1point3acres (Cloudflare CAPTCHA) and Medium all returned 403/CAPTCHA, including through a reader proxy. Reddit is not reachable by this crawler at all. Facts from those sites come only from search-result snippets or thread titles and are marked medium/low. The Medium posts by trivajay259 and anqi.silvia were paywalled.

Source reliability:
- PracHub interview-experience pages read like translated or condensed first-person reports and are reasonably reliable.
- PracHub interview-question pages, plus fastprep, trueinterview, aceoffer and The Forward Deployed, often expand short candidate reports into full specs (constraints, examples, 'what strong answers cover'). Treat exact signatures, numbers and examples there as plausible reconstructions, not verbatim Anthropic text.
- linkjob.ai, interviewfox.ai, techinterview.org, oavoservice, csoahelp and aceround are AI-assist or cheating-service marketing sites. Their claims are low confidence unless corroborated: for example the '520+/600 threshold', LLM integrity review, the 'AI-collaborative 60-75 min round' and the 'billion-doc search' prompt.

Conflicts to flag for the learner:
1. Stack-sample problem: whether to emit end events after the last sample. The canonical version says no; some variants close frames at the end. Ask the interviewer.
2. OA level visibility (hidden vs visible upfront) and count (4 vs 6 levels in 2026).
3. Coding Q-number mapping (Q1-Q6) is inconsistent across 1point3acres, hack2hire and the GitHub gist.
4. Language: the OA is reportedly Python-only while live rounds may allow other languages; the PR-review round used fixed TypeScript.
5. AI use: officially banned live and on take-homes. Exceptions are the performance take-home (explicitly allowed) and the reported 2026 Claude Code PR-review onsite. One Aced infra guide claims onsite coding can use AI tools; this is uncorroborated.
6. Timelines range from about 3 weeks to 4 months.
7. In-memory DB Level 4 varies: backup/restore, historical get, or compression in one likely-mixed report.

Staleness: Exponent/Aced Staff infra experiences (about 2024 and Sep 2025), the Taro Jan/Feb 2025 reports and the csoahelp Apr 2025 post are older. The core question families (crawler, stack trace, dedup, LRU, image pipeline, batching SD) still appear in 2026 reports, but details may have shifted.

Many CodeSignal ICA projects (in-memory DB with TTL/backup, bank system, cloud storage) are CodeSignal-provided and also reported at Meta, Coinbase, eBay and others, so non-Anthropic writeups are valid practice. Exact Anthropic level specs may differ.

The 'performance engineering take-home' is official, well documented, AI-permitted and open-sourced (anthropics/original_performance_takehome). That AI exception should not be generalized to other rounds.

Not found: no reliable reports of a 'Collaborative Prompt Playground' under that exact name (it appears as Prompt Playground / prompt-sharing platform / AI playground for very large prompts), an eval-pipeline design, or an LLM API gateway prompt from first-hand candidates. Those come only from secondary guides (spacecomplexity, oavoservice, coditioning) and should be marked low.
